userspace.party / ample

ample

Define the form once. Let its representation follow.

ample is the domain-independent grammar underneath userspace.party. It gives names, taxonomies and structures a reusable representation before any operating system, machine, person, browser or physical model gives them domain meaning.

The declaration is the source

ample's macros are not merely shorthand. They keep the definition of a concept close to the mechanics that make it usable: discriminants, constants, labels, layout information and byte conversion. A declaration becomes the source from which repeated representation code is generated. 

ample::enum_labeled!(
    pub enum Kind,
    u16,
    "kind",
    [
        [1; Header; HEADER; "HDR"; "header"],
        [2; Program; PROGRAM; "PRG"; "program"]
    ]
);

// one taxonomy
// → enum variants
// → numeric discriminants
// → named constants
// → acronyms + descriptions
// → Bytes representation

A grammar of forms

Taxonomy

enum, enum_typed, enum_labeled, enum_labeled_typed and enum_flag express finite vocabularies, their discriminants and, where appropriate, names and payloads.

Structure

struct, generic_struct and struct_tuple compose fields into reusable forms while deriving the byte mechanics from the representation of their parts.

Representation

Bytes<Origin, Destination> makes size, alignment, endianness and conversion to and from bytes explicit. Implementations extend through primitives, options, slices, tuples and pointers.

Reusable mechanics

The macro tree spans common, enums, list, primitive, string, structs, traits, tuples and unions: families of form, not application domains.

Origin is part of the representation

Serialization is not treated as an unrelated service layered on after a type exists. The representation traits carry Origin and Destination in their type story. Much of today's generated machinery is the direct Origin → Origin case, while the trait boundary preserves the distinction between where a representation comes from and where it is meant to go. 

concept

Why macros belong here

The ample side

  • Describe a general kind of structure once.
  • Generate repetitive representation mechanics from that structure.
  • Keep constants attached to the taxonomy that defines them.
  • Make nomenclature and serialization reusable across domains.

The domain side

  • Choose what the structure means in a real domain.
  • Supply ELF, Linux, human, browser or physical vocabulary.
  • Attach behavior that only makes sense inside that domain.
  • Keep platform APIs and protocols outside the abstraction layer.

The boundary

Belongs here

  • General enums, structs, tuples, unions and containers
  • Reusable taxonomy and nomenclature machinery
  • Byte representation, size, alignment and endianness traits
  • Domain-independent allocation, collections and mathematical forms

Does not belong here

  • System calls or an operating-system ABI
  • ELF as a file-format domain
  • People, colors, machines or virtual machines
  • DOM/browser concepts
  • Physical models, sensors or actuators

What this enables upstream

Example: ELF

userspace can model ELF headers, classes, machines, segment types and flags using ample's structural and enum macros. The taxonomy remains ELF's; the machinery for representing that taxonomy does not need to be reinvented inside the loader.

Start here

cargo add ample

The design rule is strict: ample may know how a form is represented, but not what an application domain says that form means.

From representation to reality. ample defines reusable forms and their representation; userspace adds process and operating-system meaning; computers describes machines; humans adds human-facing meaning; webspace owns browser presentation; and twins reaches simulation and control of the physical world.
